IINTS–AF SDK: A Safety-First Research Platform for Transparent
Diabetes Algorithm Simulation
Author Name
IINTS–AF Project

Abstract
Automated insulin delivery research needs reproducible ways to expose unsafe or unrealistic
algorithm behavior before experimental controllers are tested near real patients. IINTS–AF
SDK is an open-source research and education platform for virtual type 1 diabetes simulation,
controller benchmarking, deterministic safety supervision, data certification, clinical-style report-
ing, and physiology-aware glucose forecasting. The platform combines scenario simulation with
multiple virtual patient families, including a fast transparent custom model and ODE-based
Bergman- and Hovorka-style research models. It also models meals, exercise, stress, circadian en-
dogenous glucose production, renal glucose clearance, hypoglycemia counterregulation, glucagon
pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics, CGM lag/noise/drift/dropout, and pump-delivery im-
perfections such as dose quantization and occlusion abstractions. A central design choice is
separation of authority: algorithms may propose insulin or glucagon actions, but a deterministic
supervisor validates, reduces, or blocks proposed actions before they affect the simulated patient.
Beyond simulation, the SDK includes data preparation and MDMP-style certification workflows,
OpenAPS-style insulin-on-board and carbohydrate-on-board feature extraction, AGP-style re-
porting, explainable event logs, local AI run explanations, physics-informed glucose forecasting,
Jetson long-training workflows, and FPGA/Pi/UNO Q bench research modes. This paper
presents the system architecture, modeling assumptions, safety wrapper, AI workflow, validation
strategy, and research limitations of IINTS–AF SDK. The platform is not a medical device, not a
treatment recommendation system, and not intended for real-time patient care or clinical dosing
decisions. Its intended contribution is to make diabetes technology research more inspectable,
auditable, reproducible, and discussable before any real-world clinical deployment.
Keywords:type 1 diabetes simulation; automated insulin delivery; safety supervision; glucose
forecasting; digital patient; clinical-style reporting; reproducible research.

1 Introduction
Closed-loop diabetes technologies operate in a domain where errors can be dangerous, context-
dependent, and delayed. A controller that appears acceptable under average glucose error can still
propose unsafe insulin during falling glucose, ignore active insulin, overreact to noisy CGM values,
or fail under stress, illness, exercise, sensor dropout, or meal-absorption uncertainty. Therefore,
diabetes algorithm research needs tools that make unsafe behavior visible before algorithms are
connected to hardware or studied near real patients.
IINTS–AF SDK addresses this need as a research-only software platform for simulating virtual
diabetes scenarios and evaluating candidate algorithms under deterministic safety gates. The SDK
is designed around a simple principle:
controller proposes→safety supervisor verifies→simulator applies.
This separation prevents a neural model, LLM, PID controller, MPC controller, or custom algorithm
from directly controlling the simulated patient state. Instead, every proposed insulin or glucagon
action is checked against transparent rules and recorded with an auditable reason when it is reduced
or blocked.
The project is intentionally broader than a single simulator. It includes a command-line interface,
Python SDK, patient-model registry, algorithm registry, report generator, data import and certifi-
cation layer, glucose forecasting workflow, local explanation tools, Jetson training workflows, and
hardware-facing research modes. The main research question is whether an open-source, safety-first
simulation SDK can make risky or unrealistic diabetes-algorithm behavior visible before algorithms
are tested near real patients.
The secondary research questions are whether deterministic supervision can reduce unsafe simulated
hormone actions; whether richer physiology models improve the realism of virtual glucose traces
compared with curve-only simulators; whether AGP-style reports and explainable events make
simulation output understandable; whether physiology-aware neural losses reduce impossible glucose
forecasts; and whether local edge devices can run transparent research logic while the SDK preserves
traceability and safety boundaries.

2 Research Boundary and Contributions
IINTS–AF SDK is research and education software. It is not CE marked, FDA cleared, clinically
certified, or intended for treatment decisions. Reports are research artifacts, and no output should
be used to dose insulin or glucagon in real life. This boundary is not a minor disclaimer; it is part
of the system design. The SDK aims to support safer research discussion, not clinical deployment.
The main contributions are:
•a unified CLI and Python SDK for diabetes-algorithm simulation, data preparation, safety
validation, AI experiments, and report generation;
•a separated architecture in which candidate controllers propose actions while a deterministic
supervisor remains the hard safety gate;
•multiple virtual patient model families ranging from fast transparent simulation to physiology-
inspired ODE systems;
•CGM and pump abstractions that expose algorithms to lag, colored noise, drift, dropout,
compression lows, dose quantization, rotor-slip-style delivery error, and occlusion events;
•research models for hypoglycemia defense, HAAF memory, exogenous glucagon, exercise-driven
GLUT4/NIMGU behavior, stress, illness, renal clearance, and dawn-phenomenon dynamics;
•data workflows for CareLink, Nightscout, Tidepool, CSV, OhioT1DM, AZT1D, HUPA-UCM,
and related research datasets, including provenance manifests and MDMP-style quality checks;
•physiology-aware glucose forecasting with IOB/COB feature extraction, PINN-style penalties,
Hugging Face export, and Jetson long-training workflows;
•AGP-style reports, XAI event logs, poster/study bundles, FPGA comparison demos, and edge
workflows for bench research.

3 Related Work
The physiological side of the SDK is inspired by classic glucose-insulin modeling and closed-loop
research. Minimal-model ideas originate from Bergman-style insulin sensitivity estimation [ 1].
Richer model-predictive control and compartmental type 1 diabetes simulation are influenced by
Hovorka-style dynamics [ 2], meal simulation models [ 3], and the UVA/Padova simulator lineage
[4]. The SDK also draws on work in hypoglycemia counterregulation and HAAF [ 5,6], glucagon
pharmacokinetics and dual-hormone delivery [ 7,8], exercise physiology [ 9,10], dawn phenomenon
[11], renal glucose handling [ 12], CGM interpretation [ 13], AGP-style reporting and CGM metrics
[14], and public glucose-prediction datasets such as OhioT1DM [15].
Unlike certified simulators or clinical decision tools, IINTS–AF SDK is positioned as an inspectable
research environment. Its novelty is not a claim of clinical validity. Instead, the project combines
simulation, deterministic safety supervision, data provenance, physiology-aware AI evaluation,
explainable reporting, and bench hardware comparison into one reproducible SDK.

4 System Architecture
The SDK is organized as a reproducible pipeline from scenario description to run artifacts. A
scenario configures patient profile, meals, exercise, stress, sensor events, controller, safety settings,
random seed, and duration. At each simulation step, the patient produces latent glucose, the
sensor model converts it into a CGM-like observation, the algorithm proposes an action, the safety
supervisor checks the action, the patient model advances, and the recorder writes state, metrics,
and safety events.

5 Virtual Patient, Pump, and Sensor Modeling
5.1 Patient Model Families
The SDK contains three patient-model families. The custom model is a fast transparent simulator for
demos, regression tests, and large scenario sweeps. It tracks glucose, insulin on board, carbohydrate
on board, meal absorption, exercise state, and homeostatic drift. Its value is interpretability and
speed rather than maximum physiological detail.
The Bergman-style ODE model provides a compact mechanistic model with plasma glucose, remote
insulin action, plasma insulin, gut absorption, subcutaneous insulin depots, glucagon compartments,
glucagon action, and HAAF memory. The Hovorka-style model is the richer research engine,
separating accessible and non-accessible glucose, insulin depots, plasma insulin, insulin action
channels, meal compartments, stress/exercise pseudo-hormones, glucagon PK/PD, HAAF memory,
and exercise-driven GLUT4 activity.
Model Purpose
Custom Fast, transparent virtual patient for demos, regression tests, and large scenario
sweeps.
Bergman-style Compact ODE research model with glucose, remote insulin action, gut absorp-
tion, insulin depots, glucagon states, and HAAF memory.
Hovorka-style Rich 19-state ODE research model with multiple insulin actions, meal com-
partments, stress, exercise, glucagon, HAAF, and GLUT4/NIMGU state.

6 Deterministic Safety Supervisor
The central safety idea is separation of authority:
AI or controller proposes→supervisor verifies→simulator applies.
The supervisor is deterministic and transparent. It does not blindly trust an LLM, neural model,
PID controller, MPC controller, or custom algorithm. When a candidate action is reduced or
blocked, the simulator records the reason.

8.1 Forecasting Objective
Given a history window Xt−H:t, the model predicts a future horizon ˆYt+1:t+K . The model is evaluated
by horizon-specific error, hypoglycemia detection, physiological violations, and source/subject
splits. Inputs typically include glucose, delivered insulin, IOB, carbohydrates, COB, time of day,
activity/stress features, and source or subject identifiers.

8.3 Model Comparison Gate and Jetson AutoML Factory
The Jetson/Hugging Face training workflow promotes candidates using a lower-is-better score:
score=MAE+w physVphys+whypoVhypo,(59)
where Vphysis any-physiology-violation percentage and Vhypois missed-hypoglycemia percentage. A
candidate is not promoted simply because average error improves.
The Jetson training system is designed for low-power edge hardware. One trial runs in a child process;
failed trials are logged instead of crashing the whole run; memory is released between trials; CUDA
cache is emptied when available; thread counts are capped for Jetson stability; hyperparameters
mutate across trials; only complete candidates with metrics can become champion; and Hugging
Face warm-start training can continue from an existing model.

14 Limitations
Several limitations are central to correct interpretation. The patient models are research approxi-
mations, not personalized clinical digital twins. HAAF, counterregulation, GLP-1, GLUT4, renal
clearance, and circadian terms are experimental abstractions. Parameter values require calibration
and external validation. CGM behavior is not vendor-specific. Simulator realism depends on real
data calibration and reference envelopes.
The AI components also have limits. Low MAE does not guarantee medically useful behavior.
Subject leakage must be avoided through subject-level splits. Real patient data must not be uploaded
without explicit permission and de-identification. LLM explanations can sound plausible and must
be grounded in run artifacts.
Hardware modes are bench/research workflows. FPGA, Pico, Pi, UNO Q, and Jetson outputs
are useful for latency, determinism, and transparent-logic education, but they are not clinical
certification and must not be connected to real insulin or glucagon delivery.

15 Future Work
Future work should prioritize empirical evaluation and calibration. The most important next step
is to run the experiment suite defined above and report quantitative results without overstating
clinical meaning. Additional work should compare patient-model realism against external datasets,
extend subject-level forecasting evaluation, improve uncertainty reporting, validate report stability,
document controller-failure cases, and strengthen hardware mismatch tests. More detailed pump
and infusion-set physics could be added as a separate validated hardware model if the project later
includes material behavior, tube-wall deformation, catheter mechanics, or plunger dynamics.
Related SDK documentation that should be kept synchronized with this paper includes the archi-
tecture overview, physiology reference, hypoglycemia science model, glucose-model guide, local-AI
research notes, source library, evidence base, real-data realism guide, command reference, FPGA
mode guide, Jetson AutoML factory notes, and EU AI Pact governance notes.

16 Conclusion
IINTS–AF SDK is a reproducible, open-source research environment for making diabetes algorithm
behavior inspectable under simulated physiological stress, deterministic safety gates, data provenance
checks, clinical-style reports, and edge-case workflows. Its core insight is that diabetes algorithms
should not be evaluated only by average glucose error. They should also be evaluated by physiology,
safety interventions, data provenance, explainability, reproducibility, hardware behavior, and failure
modes. The SDK does not replace medical devices and does not provide treatment recommendations.
Its value is to support transparent pre-clinical research discussion before any algorithm is considered
for real-world clinical pathways.
