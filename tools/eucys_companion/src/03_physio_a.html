Two competing inflows and three outflows set \(dQ_1/dt\). The gut (F05) and the liver (F06 EGP) push glucose in; transport to the periphery (\(x_1 Q_1\)), insulin-independent uptake (F09) and renal loss (F14) take it out. Insulin never removes glucose directly — it only moves the rate constants of those flows, through three separate action channels with three different time constants.

i

Glucose dynamics

The central mass balance. Everything else in Part II is a term inside it.

What it represents

Glucose is tracked as a mass in two connected pools. \(Q_1\) is what a blood test would see; \(Q_2\) is glucose that has entered muscle and fat and is not immediately available. Insulin does not appear in this equation as a subtracted quantity — it appears as \(x_1\), \(x_2\) and \(x_3\), which change how fast glucose moves between pools and how hard the liver is producing. That is the essential structural claim of the model.

Why this equation

A single-pool model cannot reproduce the shape of a real excursion: it either over-predicts the peak or over-predicts the decay, because one pool has one time constant. The second pool introduces the observed delay between a glucose rise and its disposal, and it is where insulin-dependent disposal \(x_2 Q_2\) actually happens. It also makes the "insulin on board but glucose still falling" phenomenon emerge rather than being hand-coded.

Behaviour

EGP₀ ↑
Raises the fasting plateau roughly linearly. A 10% rise is ~20 mg/min, which a basal rate must fully offset or glucose drifts up all night.
k₁₂ ↑
Faster return from the periphery: shallower nadirs, slower clearance of a peak. Couples \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\) more tightly, pushing the system toward single-pool behaviour.
VG ↑
Same mass, lower concentration. Damps all excursions and makes the patient look more stable — which is why it must not be tuned to fit data.
Strongly coupled
\(x_1\) appears in both equations with opposite sign — it is a transfer, not a loss. Only \(x_2 Q_2\), NIMGU and \(F_R\) actually remove glucose from the body.
Edge case
The EGP term is clamped at zero by \(\max(0,\,\cdot)\). Once \(x_3 > 1 + x_{\mathrm{gluc}}\) the liver is fully suppressed and further insulin buys no more hepatic effect — only peripheral disposal continues.
△

Declared extensions to the published RHS. \(x_{\mathrm{gluc}}\) (glucagon), \(F_R\) (renal), the GLUT4 term inside NIMGU, the stress multiplier on EGP₀, and \(V_G\,r_{\mathrm{dawn}}\) are IINTS additions. All five default to inert, so the default configuration reduces to the published structure.

The Bergman alternative

F01Bergman-style concentration balanceadapted
$$\frac{dG}{dt}=-(p_{1,\mathrm{eff}}+X)\,G+p_{1,\mathrm{eff}}\,G_{b,\mathrm{eff}}+R_a+D_{\mathrm{dawn}}-U_{\mathrm{exercise}}-F_R$$
Concentration domain — no \(V_G\) scaling on \(F_R\) or the dawn term.

Here \(p_1\) is glucose effectiveness (insulin-independent disposal) and \(X\) is a single remote insulin action, so the minimal model collapses Hovorka's three channels into one. It is faster and has fewer identifiability problems, but it cannot separate hepatic from peripheral insulin action — which is precisely what the liver-sensitivity experiments in Chapter 8 need.

Likely jury questions

Is mass conserved in your model?

Within the tracked compartments, yes, by construction: \(x_1Q_1\) leaves \(Q_1\) and enters \(Q_2\) with identical magnitude, and \(k_{12}Q_2\) does the reverse. The genuine sinks are \(x_2Q_2\), NIMGU and \(F_R\); the sources are EGP and \(U_G\). Nothing is created by an unbalanced term — which is why a negative compartment raises rather than being clipped.

Why 0.16 L/kg for the glucose distribution volume?

It is the published Hovorka value and it is deliberately fixed. It is tempting to treat it as a fitting parameter because it scales every excursion, but doing so would let the model absorb errors from elsewhere — a too-large \(V_G\) flatters any controller by damping the peaks it failed to prevent. The docstring in the source explicitly refuses to make it dehydration- or stress-dependent for the same reason.

What happens at very low glucose — does the model go negative?

It cannot silently. Two things prevent it physiologically: NIMGU falls proportionally below 81 mg/dL (F09), so the largest sink shuts itself off, and the counter-regulatory multiplier (F11) raises EGP. If a numerical excursion still produced \(Q_1 < 0\), the step raises PatientModelDomainError and the model rolls back. The simulator separately terminates a run held below 40 mg/dL for 30 minutes.

CHAPTER 5

Subcutaneous insulin absorption

Why a bolus given now is still acting in four hours.

F04adaptedtwo serial depots
F04Two-depot subcutaneous absorption
$$\begin{aligned} \frac{dS_1}{dt}&=u_I-\frac{S_1}{t_{\max,I}}\\ \frac{dS_2}{dt}&=\frac{S_1}{t_{\max,I}}-\frac{S_2}{t_{\max,I}}\\ U_I&=\frac{S_2}{t_{\max,I}} \end{aligned}$$
Hovorka uses \(1/t_{\max,I}\); Bergman uses an explicit \(k_a\). Both are the same two-state chain.

What it represents

Insulin injected subcutaneously does not enter the bloodstream immediately. It must dissociate from its hexameric form and diffuse through tissue. Two identical first-order compartments in series produce a gamma-shaped appearance curve — zero rate at the instant of injection, a smooth rise, a single peak, a long tail — which matches measured PK far better than a single exponential, whose maximum rate would be at \(t=0\).

Why this equation

This chain is the physical origin of insulin-on-board. Because it is a real state and not a bookkeeping estimate, dose stacking emerges automatically: a second bolus given 45 minutes after the first arrives while \(S_2\) from the first is still near its peak. Any controller that ignores this will hypo, and the bench will show it.

Behaviour

tmax,I ↑
Later, lower, broader peak. Total insulin delivered is unchanged — the area under \(U_I\) always equals the dose. Slower insulin is not less insulin.
tmax,I ↓
Sharper peak, shorter tail. Easier to time against a meal, less forgiving of a mistimed bolus.
Analytic peak
For a bolus, \(U_I\) peaks at exactly \(t = t_{\max,I}\) — which is what the parameter name means. Plasma insulin peaks later because elimination and distribution add a further lag.
Edge case
Both depots use the same constant. This is a deliberate simplification: a two-parameter chain would fit PK data better but is not identifiable from the inputs the SDK accepts.

Derivation of the initial condition

The model must start in a fasting steady state, or every run begins with a large artificial transient. At steady state under constant basal input \(u_I^{basal}\):

$$\begin{aligned} \text{set } \frac{dS_1}{dt}=0 &\;\Rightarrow\; u_I^{basal}=\frac{S_1}{t_{\max,I}} \;\Rightarrow\; S_1^{*}=u_I^{basal}\,t_{\max,I}\\ \text{set } \frac{dS_2}{dt}=0 &\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{S_1}{t_{\max,I}}=\frac{S_2}{t_{\max,I}} \;\Rightarrow\; S_2^{*}=S_1^{*}\\ \text{then } U_I^{*}&=\frac{S_2^{*}}{t_{\max,I}}=u_I^{basal} \end{aligned}$$
Each step is a stationarity condition, valid because the input is constant; the last line confirms mass balance — appearance equals infusion.

Implementation · hovorka_model.py:_default_ode_state sets \(S_1 = S_2 = u_I^{basal}\,t_{\max,I}\) exactly as derived above.

Likely jury questions

Why two compartments and not three, or a full diffusion model?

Two is the smallest chain that gives zero initial rate and a single interior peak, which is the qualitative shape measured PK has. A third compartment adds a parameter that cannot be identified from the inputs this SDK accepts (a dose and an insulin type), so it would be a free parameter dressed as physiology. The published Hovorka model makes the same choice.

Does injection site, temperature or exercise change absorption here?

No, and that is a stated limitation. All three demonstrably affect absorption in vivo. Modelling them would require a site-and-perfusion-dependent \(t_{\max,I}\) that this SDK has no data to calibrate, so instead the insulin type is a discrete, documented choice. Exercise does affect the model, but through glucose uptake (F09) and sensitivity (F08), not through absorption.

One state, one elimination constant — and the reference point that makes insulin action signed.

What it represents

A single well-mixed insulin pool cleared at a constant fractional rate. The insulin half-life that follows is \(\ln 2 / k_e \approx 5.0\) minutes — which is short, and correctly so: the long tail of an insulin bolus comes almost entirely from the subcutaneous depots, not from slow plasma clearance. Confusing the two is a common modelling error.

Why the reference insulin matters

In the Bergman backend, insulin action is driven by \(I - I_{\mathrm{ref}}\), a deviation from a reference rather than an absolute level. \(I_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is derived from the configured basal delivery, distribution volume and clearance:

$$I_{\mathrm{ref}}=\frac{u_I^{basal}}{V_I\,k_e}$$
The configured \(I_b\) is used only when basal delivery is zero.

A direct consequence: \(X\) can go negative. When plasma insulin falls below the level the programmed basal sustains — after a suspend, say — remote action becomes negative and glucose rises faster than \(p_1\) alone would predict. This is intended and documented in the registry; it is not a sign error.

△

The graft term is an abstraction, not a cure model. \(M_{\mathrm{graft}}\) is a dimensionless survival fraction decaying at a configurable immune-rejection rate. A value of 100% is explicitly not a clinical cure claim. Evidence class: heuristic. It is disabled by default in every T1D profile.

Likely jury questions

A 5-minute insulin half-life sounds far too short.

It is correct for plasma insulin itself — endogenous insulin clearance is genuinely fast. What people mean by "insulin lasts four hours" is the duration of action, which in this model is produced by two other mechanisms: the subcutaneous depots keep feeding \(U_I\) for hours (F04), and the action states \(x_1, x_2, x_3\) have their own much slower time constants (F07) — \(1/k_{a1}\) alone is 167 minutes. Plasma insulin is the fast link between two slow ones.

You model type 1 diabetes, so why is there a secretion term at all?

It exists solely for the regenerative-medicine research branch — simulating a partially functional islet graft. It is gated behind the Bergman backend, its gain defaults to zero, and the patient factory raises an error if graft parameters are passed to a backend that does not support them, rather than silently ignoring them.
